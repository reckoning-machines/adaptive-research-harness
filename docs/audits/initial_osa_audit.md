## Executive disposition

**B — BOUNDED DESIGN GAPS WITHIN A COHERENT FRAME.**

The draft has one owner per governed fact, one commit boundary per ledger,
a classification for every carrier it names, and an answer to every one of
the twelve challenges. It is not constitutionally coherent yet. Twelve
questions remain open, five of them consequential (D-03, D-07, D-08, D-10,
D-11). None can be resolved by writing more prose; each needs a decision by
the Principal, and two (D-07, D-08) need a decision before step 4 of the
plan can begin.

Thresholds used:

- **A — COHERENT** would require every identity, meaning, owner, commit
  boundary and currentness rule to be determinable from the draft alone,
  with no TENTATIVE marker.
- **B — BOUNDED GAPS** applies when the frame is sound and the gaps are
  enumerable, each with a deciding authority.
- **C — STRUCTURAL GAP** would apply if any governed fact had two owners,
  no owner, or an owner that is an agent, cache or evaluation.

## Established principles versus tentative design choices

**Established** (adopted from fin123-core, or stated in the founding brief
as a premise; not open for redesign in this phase):

- Constitutional Coherence, OSA, Authority Normal Form, the six
  representation classes, the seven authority invariants.
- CPOC's exact meaning and subordination.
- The Constitutional Delta `ΔO/ΔS/ΔA` with a normal budget of `0/0/0`.
- Commitment precedes outcome; two evidentiary classes never summed;
  successors begin at zero.
- The nine distinctions in the brief (belief vs capital vs contribution;
  fitness is not profitability; lineage vs economic identity; evidence does
  not transfer; profit does not prove the hypothesis; speculative Theme,
  testable Hypothesis, executable Strategy; historical vs prospective).
- Independent verification for consequential admission.
- An agent may write a successor but may not grant it authority or change

## Defect register

Each defect: the OSA dimension threatened, a concrete example, the deciding
authority, and standing.

### D-01. Theme identity under revision — OPEN (Ontology)

Example: Theme `TH-7` "dealer positioning in index options" is broadened to
include single-name options. Is that a successor Theme (new `ThemeId`,
Hypotheses re-parented?) or an amendment? The draft says successor, which
forces every Hypothesis under `TH-7` to be re-parented by Transition or left
under a retired Theme. Neither is obviously right. Deciding authority:
Principal, at plan step 1.

### D-02. Evidentiary class when a target period is partially resolved — OPEN (Semantics)

Example: Hypothesis `HY-88` is registered at sequence 2 310 on 2026-10-18.
A Prediction targets the window 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22. Three of the six
sessions have resolved. The draft's rule (registration sequence versus
period resolution) gives no answer for a straddling window. Candidate
rules: the window is Historical Reconstruction if any part resolved; or the
Prediction is refused. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 2.

### D-03. Materiality of an Economic Proposition change — OPEN, CONSEQUENTIAL (Semantics, Authority)

Example: `SV-5.4` changes rebalancing from daily to weekly and the proposer
declares `Proposition delta: NONE`. The Checker believes turnover and
capacity have changed enough that the strategy now exploits a different
inefficiency. The draft routes disputes to the Principal, but gives no
criterion the Checker can apply, so every declaration is in effect decided
by whoever argues better. The threat is challenge 3: silent meaning change
with inherited record. Candidate repair: the Economic Proposition must list
its own invariants (horizon band, universe, mechanism, turnover band) at
registration, and `NONE` is lawful only if every listed invariant holds.
Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 2.

### D-04. Ownership of "standing" — REPAIRED (Authority)

The candidate list gave Hypothesis, Strategy and Agent Design each an owner
and also gave Population State "assessments", which risked two owners for
standing. The draft now states that object content is owned by the
immutable record and standing is owned exclusively by Population State
through Apply; assessments are `DERIVED` Evaluations. Repaired in
§III.2, §III.3, §III.4, §III.6.

### D-05. Resolution source revision — OPEN (Semantics, Currentness)

Example: Prediction `PR-501` resolves against source S's daily gamma
exposure. S restates the 2026-10-20 value a week later. The Outcome was
committed against the first value. The draft says Outcomes are immutable
and Market Observations are `RECEIPT`s, so the Outcome stands. But nothing
says whether a second Outcome may be appended, whether the Evaluation
Standard must use the first, or whether the Prediction must declare "first
print" or "final". Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 2.

### D-06. Evidentiary continuity claim — OPEN (Semantics)

Example: `SV-5.4` is admitted with a checked continuity claim to `SV-5.3`.
A later Allocation Transition cites "lineage SL-5 forward Sharpe over 18
months". The draft requires per-version reporting and records the claim,
but does not say whether an Allocation may be *decided* on the claimed
combined record. If yes, the claim has acquired a decision right, which I-13
forbids for anything not authoritative. If no, the claim mechanism is
decorative. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 2. The strict
alternative (no claim mechanism) is simpler and should be the default
unless a concrete use is shown.

### D-07. Authority of history: Transition sequence versus state versions — OPEN, CONSEQUENTIAL (Authority, Recovery)

The draft makes the admitted Transition sequence authoritative and every
materialized Population State `DERIVED`. Consequence: recovery is replay,
and the current state is "the result of applying everything admitted".
Example of the risk: a Constitutional Transition at sequence 500 changes
how an Allocation is interpreted. Replaying Transition 300 under the new
Constitution would produce a different state 300 than existed at the time.
The draft does not say whether replay uses the Constitution version
current at each Transition (it must, but that rule is unstated). The
alternative, state versions as the authority with Transitions as
`RECEIPT`s, avoids the replay-interpretation problem but makes the "state"
a mutable-looking object whose history is a chain of snapshots. Deciding
authority: Principal, before plan step 4.

### D-08. The clock — OPEN, CONSEQUENTIAL (Semantics, Authority)

I-2 relies on commit sequence numbers assigned by the commit boundary.
Forward-class determination relies on "the period had not resolved". Those
are two different orderings: the system's sequence and world time. Example:
source S publishes 2026-10-20 gamma data at 18:00 on 2026-10-20. A
Prediction for 2026-10-20 committed at 17:30 is forward by wall clock. A
Prediction committed at 18:30 is not, even though no Outcome has been
committed yet, because the answer was knowable. The draft says "before the
outcome is knowable" but names no authority for "knowable". Candidate
repair: each resolution source declares its publication schedule as part of
Hypothesis registration, and the commit boundary records wall time from one
declared clock. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 2.

### D-09. Paper Book as a tenth object — OPEN (Ontology)

The draft adds it with justification (allocation and position are different
facts). The alternative folds orders into Population State, which would make
Apply the writer of execution facts it did not decide. I recommend keeping
the Paper Book. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 1.

### D-10. Sufficiency of Checker independence — OPEN, CONSEQUENTIAL (Authority)

Example: proposer `AD-3.3` and checker `AD-9.1` are different lineages,
different Authorizations, same underlying model, same prompt style, same
blind spots. The draft's definition is satisfied and the check is not
independent in any useful sense. Candidate strengthening: a consequential
check by an agent requires a different model provider or a human; or
Checker Agent Designs must be registered under a Checker-only lineage that
never proposes. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 5.

### D-11. The Principal bottleneck — OPEN, CONSEQUENTIAL (Authority)

Every consequential Transition is decided by one human. A research process
that registers dozens of Hypotheses a day will stall. The draft's "admitted
policy" slot is deliberately empty. The question is which Transition
classes can lawfully be decided by policy first (likely Hypothesis
registration within budget and Theme), what evidence a policy needs before
admission, and how policy decisions remain attributable. Deciding
authority: Principal, plan step 5.

### D-12. Budget reservation — OPEN (Concurrency)

Example: two agents each plan 40 units of research against a Theme with 60
remaining. Both run, both commit Research Records, the second Transition is
refused at Apply for insufficient budget, and 40 units of work are lost.
Either reservation exists at proposal (a new governed fact with its own
owner and expiry) or research effort is wasted by design. Deciding
authority: Principal, plan step 4.

### D-13. Lineage memory as a writer of successors — REPAIRED (Authority)

I-5 says a successor "carries" lineage memory. Read literally, that makes
lineage memory a fact written into the successor record by the proposer.
Repaired wording: lineage memory is a `DERIVED` view over the Research
Record ledger keyed by `AgentDesignLineageId` and `ThemeId`; no record
carries it, so no successor can omit it. Repaired in §III.4 and I-5.

### D-14. Provider substitution and Agent Design identity — OPEN (Ontology)

An Agent Design Version's content hash includes its instructions and tools.
Does it include the model provider and model version? If yes, every
provider update retires every agent. If no, a provider change silently
changes behavior under one identity, which is challenge 3 for agents.
fin123-core treats substitution as a governed event; the draft is silent.
Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 1.

### D-15. Two carriers of remaining research budget — REPAIRED (Authority)

The draft listed "remaining research budgets" in Population State content
while Authorization also carried a budget. Two carriers of one fact.
Repaired: the Authorization owns the grant (immutable ceiling); the
Transition sequence owns consumption; remaining is `DERIVED`. Repaired in
§III.6 and §III.7.

### D-16. "Evaluation-driven proposal" — REPAIRED (Authority)

The Transition class table listed the proposer of a standing change as
"Evaluation-driven proposal". An Evaluation is `DERIVED` and cannot
propose. Repaired: the proposer is an authorized agent or the Principal,
citing the Evaluation by its `StateVersion` and standard version.

### D-17. Reconstructing a Transition interrupted between Check and Apply — OPEN (Recovery)

Example: `TR-340` is CHECKED PASS and ADMITTED by the Principal; the
process dies before Apply. On recovery, is the admission durable? The draft
says lifecycle standing is "appended events", which implies yes if the
admission event was committed. But the draft does not say whether a
Decision is itself a commit on the Transition ledger or a step inside
Apply. If the latter, an admitted-but-unapplied Transition cannot exist and
the Principal must decide again, which is a mutable-present substitution
risk. Deciding authority: Principal, plan step 4; this is the first CPOC
witness to design.

## The twelve challenges

For each: invariant threatened; evidence that would expose it; authority
that decides what happens next; state that must survive recovery.

### 1. Two agents create competing authorities

Example: `AD-3.3` maintains a "current hypothesis register" file and `AD-9.1`
maintains another; each reads its own to decide what to propose next, and
they diverge.

- Threatened: ANF singular decision authority; I-6; I-13.
- Exposed by: a Transition citing a Hypothesis standing that differs from
  Population State at its declared base; any file or store an agent writes
  that is not a Transition proposal or Evidence commit.
- Decides: nothing to decide. Neither register is authoritative, because no
  Authorization can create authority (I-6). Both are `EPHEMERAL`; proposals
  built on them are checked against Population State and fail if wrong.
- Survives: Population State and the Evidence ledger. The agents' files
  need not survive.

### 2. Two individually lawful strategies create an unlawful combined portfolio

Example: constitution §VIII, sequence diagram. `TR-340` and `TR-341` each
fit the gross limit alone; together they exceed it.

- Threatened: I-9, I-10, Population State limits.
- Exposed by: the composed check at the actual base; a resulting state that
  violates a limit.
- Decides: Check and Apply, which are OSA admission functions. The second
  Transition is REJECTED and preserved. The Principal may re-propose a
  smaller allocation.
- Survives: both Transition records with both check results. This is not a
  CPOC question: no process died. CPOC would only ask whether a fresh
  process reaches the same refusal from the ledgers, which it does.

### 3. A strategy mutation silently changes its economic proposition

Example: `SV-5.5` keeps the name and lineage, replaces the signal, declares
`Proposition delta: NONE`.

- Threatened: Strategy economic identity (§III.3); I-4.
- Exposed by: the Checker comparing the rule change with the proposition
  invariants (D-03 makes this checkable); a continuity claim on a version
  whose proposition invariants fail.
- Decides: Checker refuses; Principal decides a dispute. If admitted as
  `NEW`, `SV-5.5` binds a new `EconomicPropositionId` and starts at zero.
- Survives: both proposition records, the Checker's finding, the per-version
  records.

### 4. An agent alters its evaluation rules to improve its measured performance

Example: `AD-3.3` proposes an Evaluation that scores Agent Design
contribution with a new weighting favoring the kind of research it does.

- Threatened: I-7; Constitution ownership of Evaluation Standards.
- Exposed by: an Evaluation naming an `EvaluationStandardVersion` that is
  not the one in the current Population State; any Transition touching the
  standard proposed by a non-Principal.
- Decides: the Evaluation is `DERIVED` and simply not the standard's output;
  it has no effect on standing. A proposal to change the standard is a
  Constitutional Transition, STOPPED until the Principal decides, with
  `ΔS ≠ 0` declared.
- Survives: the Constitution version sequence; every Evaluation's
  standard-version binding.

### 5. A hypothesis is rewritten after seeing its outcome

Example: `HY-88` predicted variance exceeds implied by Y; it did not; the
agent "clarifies" the threshold to Y/2 under the same id.

- Threatened: Hypothesis immutability; I-2; I-4.
- Exposed by: any write to an existing `HypothesisId`; a Prediction whose
  commit sequence is greater than its Outcome's; a successor Hypothesis
  claiming the predecessor's record.
- Decides: the commit boundary refuses the write. A successor `HY-89` may
  be registered with zero record and an explicit predecessor link. The
  failed Prediction remains on `HY-88`.
- Survives: `HY-88`, `PR-501`, its Outcome, and `HY-89` with its link.

### 6. A descendant claims its ancestor's prospective evidence

Example: `SV-5.4` is allocated on the strength of `SV-5.3`'s 18-month
forward record.

- Threatened: I-4; evidentiary class closure.
- Exposed by: an Allocation Transition whose justification cites a record
  bound to a different `StrategyVersionId` without a checked continuity
  claim; any report summing records across versions.
- Decides: Checker refuses the citation. Under the strict alternative
  (D-06) there is nothing to claim. Under the draft, a checked claim may be
  recorded, and whether it may be acted on is D-06.
- Survives: per-version records, the claim record and its check.

### 7. A research agent resets its search history by creating another agent

Example: `AD-3.3` has spent 340 units on Theme `TH-7` with eight negative
results; it proposes `AD-3.4` and intends to re-run the same searches.

- Threatened: I-5; research budget.
- Exposed by: lineage memory, which is a `DERIVED` view over Research
  Records keyed by lineage and Theme and cannot be omitted (D-13); a
  Research Record repeating a closed question.
- Decides: the Checker sees the lineage history. Whether repeating a closed
  question is refused or merely visible is a Research Authorization term
  the Principal sets. The budget was consumed from the Authorization and
  does not return.
- Survives: Research Records, the lineage link, Authorization consumption.

### 8. A cache or materialized view becomes authoritative

Example: a materialized Population State at 46 is served to the Checker
while the sequence has advanced to 47; the Checker passes a Transition at
base 46.

- Threatened: ANF; I-9; I-13.
- Exposed by: Apply refusing a stale base; cross-surface disagreement after
  restart (a CPOC negative invariant) if the materialization is read as
  truth after a fresh start.
- Decides: nothing. The stale check is useless; the Transition is
  re-checked at 47. A reader that cannot reach the sequence refuses.
- Survives: the sequence. The materialization is discarded and rebuilt.

### 9. A process crashes between a paper allocation decision and an order

Constitution §VI T-D.

- Threatened: CPOC continuation; I-8; I-12.
- Exposed by: an applied Allocation Transition with no bound order after a
  fresh start; or two orders with the same idempotency key.
- Decides: the continuation is reconstructed, not re-decided. The execution
  function emits the one missing order. The Paper Book refuses a duplicate
  key.
- Survives: the Transition sequence through the applied Allocation, and the
  Paper Book. D-17 is the companion case one step earlier.

### 10. A retired object is accidentally restored during recovery

Example: a recovery procedure loads a materialized state from 44, where
`SV-5.3` was still allocated, after the sequence has reached 46 where it is
retired.

- Threatened: I-11; CPOC resurrection invariant.
- Exposed by: a position or allocation for an object whose latest
  Transition is a retirement; the materialization's `StateVersion` being
  lower than the sequence head.
- Decides: nothing. The materialization is `DERIVED` and discarded. Recovery
  is replay to the head (D-07 governs how).
- Survives: the sequence including the retirement.

### 11. A research agent discovers that its work requires a wider ΔA budget

Example: `AD-3.3` finds that its best Strategy needs to read an execution
cost model that only the Paper Book can own, and proposes to let the agent
write cost estimates into the Paper Book.

- Threatened: I-6; I-8; constitutional budget `0/0/0`.
- Exposed by: a Transition whose declared `ΔA` is non-zero, or whose
  mutation writes a ledger the Authorization does not cover.
- Decides: the Transition is STOPPED and preserved (I-14). The Principal may
  grant a new Authorization or make a Constitutional Transition. The agent
  does not continue by approximating.
- Survives: the STOPPED Transition with its stated need.

### 12. Two concurrent transitions consume the same capital or research budget

Example: constitution §VIII for capital; D-12 for research.

- Threatened: I-9; I-10; budget ownership.
- Exposed by: a stale base at Apply; a composed check failing a limit.
- Decides: Apply refuses the second; it is re-checked and admitted or
  rejected. For research budget the work may already be spent (D-12).
- Survives: both Transition records, consumption as applied.

## What this audit does not establish

It does not establish that the draft is coherent, admitted or ready for
implementation. It establishes that the gaps are enumerable, each has a
deciding authority, and none is being resolved by imagined code.
