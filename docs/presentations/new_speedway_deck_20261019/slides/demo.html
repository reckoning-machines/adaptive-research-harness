What we would demonstrate first.

Give Speedway an investment question and a research budget. Watch it propose competing tests, run them, compare the evidence and recommend the next experiment. Inspect every result and every dollar spent.

Give it a question and a budget

An investment question and a research budget

It proposes competing tests

Several ways to test the idea, each with a stated reason

It runs them

On historical data, through an existing backtester

It compares the evidence

What held up, what failed, what it cost

It recommends the next experiment

Chosen from the evidence, not from a hunch

What it proves

The research loop works end to end, every result and every dollar spent is inspectable, and the evidence changes what is asked next.

What it does not prove

An edge. Replay rejects bad ideas. Only forward results can support one.

Underneath: independent checks on every change, and recovery from the record if a run is interrupted. Design admitted on paper, October 2026. No code, no live data, no capital.

Lead with the promise, not the mechanics. The first demonstration runs about ten research agents against one existing backtester on historical and synthetic data, with a bounded synthetic model provider so the budget controls can be shown end to end. Recovery after interruption and the independent check on each change support the promise; mention them if asked. Do not claim that external provider calls can never be repeated; what is guaranteed is that no charge is counted twice and that reconstruction comes from the record. It exercises the research loop, not trading. A second demonstration covers forward research from a synthetic market development. Paper execution comes later under a separate grant.
