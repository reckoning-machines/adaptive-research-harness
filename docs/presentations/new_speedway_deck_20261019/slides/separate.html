A profitable strategy can have the wrong explanation.

So the explanation, the trade and the research method are judged separately, each on its own record.

The hypothesis

Was the explanation supported?

Judged by its own predictions, committed before the outcome was knowable.

The strategy

Did the trade make money after costs?

Judged on its own forward record, net of modelled costs, within risk limits.

The research method

Did the researcher produce useful work?

Judged on research quality for the budget, never on a count of appealing backtests.

An agent proposes a better research method. We compare it with its predecessor on new tasks, including the cost.

A right explanation can lose money. A winning trade can rest on a wrong one. Speedway records which, and acts on each.

No score in one competition feeds another unless a recorded decision says so.

Three populations, three questions. In the design, hypotheses compete for belief, strategies compete for paper capital, and research agents compete on contribution per unit of budget. The separation is a rule, not a habit: a strategy citing a hypothesis transfers no standing to it, and a hypothesis gains standing only from its own committed predictions.
